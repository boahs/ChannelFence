ChannelFence vs. BlockTube

ChannelFence combines a persistent creator block list with optional Home cleanup and smaller thumbnails. BlockTube offers more advanced filtering rules. Here is how to choose the right fit for the YouTube feed you want.

Last reviewed November 3, 2026 · ChannelFence 0.2.8 and BlockTube 0.4.8. Features can change after this date.

Firefox availability updated November 8, 2026: ChannelFence 0.2.9 is now available for desktop Firefox.

ChannelFence's live YouTube checks run on separate schedules: Chrome twice daily and Firefox daily.

From the developer of ChannelFence

I built ChannelFence, so this is my perspective on why you might choose it. The comparison uses ChannelFence's source and tests, and BlockTube's official documentation. It is a comparison of documented features, not a head-to-head performance or live compatibility benchmark.

Why I would choose ChannelFence for everyday blocking

ChannelFence is designed around a simple action: block a creator where you encounter them, then keep browsing. Your saved list applies across supported Home feeds, search, recommendations, Watch Next, Shorts, playlists, comments, and direct pages.

  • Block without leaving the feed. Use inline creator controls or supported video and compact Shorts menus. In the Shorts viewer, block the current creator and continue to the next Short.
  • Keep control of your list. Undo a block, manage creators in settings, pause blocking, or export and import a private backup. Collaboration videos can be hidden when YouTube identifies a blocked creator.
  • Make Home less cluttered. Hide supported sponsored cards, promotional banners, Explore more topics, and other optional inserts. Choose Small, Medium, or Large thumbnails for the compact grid.
  • Use a familiar language. ChannelFence has 18 interface languages, including Korean, Japanese, Brazilian Portuguese, and Arabic with right-to-left layouts. YouTube keeps its own language setting.

The Home controls start off, so you choose what changes. They do not skip video ads or sponsored Shorts in the Shorts viewer. See the Home cleanup guide and language guide for the exact controls.

When BlockTube is the better fit

BlockTube's documented strengths matter if you want rules that go beyond identifying creators:

  • You need to filter video titles, runtime, keywords, video IDs, or comments by content.
  • You want regular expressions or custom JavaScript-based blocking rules.

Those capabilities are described in the official BlockTube listing and README. ChannelFence does not provide those advanced rule editors.

YouTube changes. ChannelFence is tested to keep up.

ChannelFence schedules automated checks against real YouTube twice a day for Chrome and daily for Firefox, alongside regression tests for updates. When a covered workflow breaks, a failed test gives me a signal to investigate. You can inspect the Chrome live YouTube test runs, Firefox live YouTube test runs, and regression results.

My aim is to catch and fix compatibility problems before you notice them. Tests cannot cover every YouTube layout or account variation, and a fix still needs to reach users through an extension update. If something slips through, report the example so it can be investigated.

Privacy without an account

ChannelFence is free and open source. Your settings and block list stay in your browser's local extension storage, with no extension analytics or developer-controlled server. When a nonempty block list needs a compact Short's creator, ChannelFence may request public metadata directly from YouTube without account credentials. See the privacy policy for details.
